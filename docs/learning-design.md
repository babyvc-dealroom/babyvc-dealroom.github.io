# Learning design

The Deal Room teaches by consequence: players sign terms, then live with them for the rest of the game. Around that core, a light learning layer explains what they just met, at the moment they meet it.

## Principles

1. **Explain at the point of need.** Every acronym and concept in cards, sheets, envelopes and tips is clickable (`AutoTerms`): SAFE, MFN, ESOP, ROFR, ROFN, NRR, BATNA, ZOPA and 60 more.
2. **Numbers from the game, not from a textbook.** Each concept comes with a worked example computed by the same engine that runs the cap table.
3. **One idea at a time.** A round opens with one concept; the others wait in the Learn tab.
4. **Strategy, not just vocabulary.** The coach and the Strategies section teach negotiation habits: BATNA, interests over positions, packages over single levers, anchoring with a reason, give to get, thinking in euros and two rounds ahead.
5. **Reflection after action.** The debrief brings two quiz questions with explanations; the facilitator has debrief questions in the solution.
6. **Rewards that do not distort the game.** Badges celebrate good habits (anchoring, explaining a counter-offer, checking the Journey, signing within limits) but give no points.

## Where each piece appears

| Moment | Players see |
|---|---|
| First entry | Four-line tour for their role |
| Read phase | «Concept of the round» popup with a live example; coach tip |
| Negotiation | Coach tips on anchoring and trading; walk-away warnings; impact preview two rounds ahead |
| Incoming offer | Coach tip on not accepting the first offer |
| Signing | Ceremony, then the relationship vote |
| Debrief | Score reveal, quiz, leaderboard |
| Any time | Learn tab: concepts, strategies, searchable glossary, badges, sources |
